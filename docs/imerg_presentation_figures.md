# IMERG diagnostic decoder — presentation figure set

Figures for the three IMERG inference runs, with the caption and the numbers to say out loud for
each one. Generated by [`scripts/imerg_presentation_figures.py`](../scripts/imerg_presentation_figures.py);
output lands in `plots/imerg_presentation/` as PNG (300 dpi, for slides) and PDF (vector, for print).

> These are the **presentation** figures: a curated set with a narrative. For the internal
> `weathergen.evaluate` output — 16 metrics × 4 regions × every lead, in the package's own
> styling — see [`imerg_evaluation_configs.md`](imerg_evaluation_configs.md). Both are driven by
> the same matched-initialisation lists, so their numbers agree.

```bash
# everything, on a compute node -- the map figures and the parameter counts are the heavy part
srun --account=e-ext-2025e01-128 --partition=booster --nodes=1 --time=00:40:00 \
  env MPLBACKEND=Agg uv run --offline python scripts/imerg_presentation_figures.py --figure all

# a single figure, cheap enough to iterate on interactively
uv run --offline python scripts/imerg_presentation_figures.py --figure F06

# re-derive the 37 matched initialisations from the stores and check the pinned lists
uv run --offline python scripts/imerg_presentation_figures.py --verify-matched-inits
```

Two slow steps are cached as dotfiles in the output directory: the automatic case-study selection
(`.case_<run>_*.json`) and the checkpoint parameter counts (`.params_<parent>.json`). Delete them to
force re-derivation — re-reading the 3–5 GB checkpoints is most of T01's runtime.

## The three runs

| run | backbone | parent | rollout in store | initialisations used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `xfaps4wq` | Forecast pretraining (`c71eo6pu`) | `hmpa42d2` | 2 steps (+6 h, +12 h) | 37 matched |
| `r15j90ns` | JEPA / SSL pretraining (`af90zz71`) | `nescpnb2` | 2 steps (+6 h, +12 h) | 37 matched |
| `z71y2ik8` | JEPA / SSL pretraining (`af90zz71`) | `v3gs9fwn` | 40 steps (+6 h … +240 h) | 10 |

In all three the backbone is frozen — `freeze_modules` covers the encoder, forecast engine and
latent heads — and only a fresh IMERG decoder is trained, with MSE on IMERG `tp` (N320, 6 h windows).

## Part 1 — setup

### `F01_lineage`

Schematic of how the runs relate: the two pretrained backbones on top, the shared frozen-backbone
IMERG-decoder recipe in the middle, the finetuned checkpoints and the inference runs below.

**Say:** every run trains only the IMERG decoder; the backbone never moves.

**The caveat this slide exists to plant:** the two backbones differ in *far more* than the
pretraining objective — 339 vs 126 input channels, N320 vs o96 analysis input, 2048×2 vs 1024×4
local encoder, 0 vs 64 register tokens, no vs yes cross-stream attention. The 2-step result is
"which pretrained model gives the better precipitation decoder", **not** a controlled
SSL-vs-supervised ablation. Say this before someone else does.

### `T01_model_table`

Size, architecture and budget for all three runs. Also written as
`plots/imerg_presentation/T01_model_table.md` for pasting into a document.

| | Forecast (`xfaps4wq`) | JEPA (`r15j90ns`) | JEPA 8-step (`z71y2ik8`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pretrained backbone | `hmpa42d2` | `nescpnb2` | `v3gs9fwn` |
| Total parameters | 840 M | 1164 M | 1164 M |
| — encoder | 289 M | 613 M | 613 M |
| — forecast engine | 537 M | 537 M | 537 M |
| — IMERG decoder | 13.8 M | 13.8 M | 13.8 M |
| Trainable in finetune | 13.8 M (1.6%) | 13.8 M (1.2%) | 13.8 M (1.2%) |
| Local encoder | 2048 × 2 | 1024 × 4 | 1024 × 4 |
| Global encoder | 2048 × 4 | 2048 × 5 | 2048 × 5 |
| Register tokens | 0 | 64 | 64 |

**Say:** the forecast engine is identical in size across all three (537 M); the size difference is

frozen pretrained backbone, which is the whole point of the diagnostic-decoder approach.

[`imerg_finetune_parents_comparison.md`](imerg_finetune_parents_comparison.md) §2 for `af90zz71`
(1162.8 M total / 613.2 encoder / 537.0 forecast engine), and it does — the small total difference
is the IMERG decoder (13.8 M) standing in for the ERA5 one (12.7 M).

## Part 2 — two steps, head to head

All Part 2 figures use the **37 initialisations shared by both runs**
(2023-06-01T12 → 2023-06-29T18, 6-hourly).

> **Why 37 and not 64.** The two inference runs emitted their initialisations in *different
> orders*: sample index 1 is 2023-06-06T18 in `xfaps4wq` and 2023-06-04T12 in `r15j90ns`. The
> previously circulated comparison applied `sample: "0-63"` to both and so averaged the two runs
> over **different sets of forecasts**. Only 37 of those 64 initialisations are shared, and these
> figures use exactly those. This makes the comparison paired, which is what allows the bootstrap
> confidence intervals on the differences.

### `F02_2step_scorecard`

Three panels of absolute skill with bootstrap CIs, plus a forest plot of the paired difference
oriented so that **right of zero always means JEPA better**.

| metric | lead | Forecast | JEPA | difference | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ETS | +6 h | 0.5178 | 0.5150 | −0.0028 | [−0.0042, −0.0014] |
| ETS | +12 h | 0.4743 | 0.4790 | **+0.0048** | [+0.0033, +0.0061] |
| SEEPS skill | +6 h | 0.6465 | 0.6478 | +0.0013 | [−0.0005, +0.0030] |
| SEEPS skill | +12 h | 0.6184 | 0.6234 | **+0.0050** | [+0.0032, +0.0067] |
| FBI | +6 h | 1.110 | 1.134 | +0.0248 | [+0.0203, +0.0290] |
| FBI | +12 h | 1.200 | 1.214 | +0.0136 | [+0.0080, +0.0189] |
| RMSE (mm/6h) | +6 h | 2.136 | 2.116 | −0.021 | [−0.038, −0.001] |
| RMSE (mm/6h) | +12 h | 2.484 | 2.395 | **−0.088** | [−0.107, −0.072] |
| MAE (mm/6h) | +6 h | 0.5009 | 0.5040 | +0.0031 | [+0.0018, +0.0045] |
| MAE (mm/6h) | +12 h | 0.5747 | 0.5675 | −0.0073 | [−0.0096, −0.0050] |
| Bias (mm/6h) | +6 h | −0.0419 | −0.0249 | +0.0169 | [+0.0133, +0.0206] |

**Say:** the two backbones are within ~1% of each other everywhere. The Forecast backbone is very
slightly ahead at +6 h; by +12 h the JEPA backbone is ahead on ETS, SEEPS and RMSE — every
difference is significant on 37 paired initialisations, and every difference is small.

**The framing that makes this interesting:** the JEPA backbone reaches that with **126 input
channels on an o96 analysis** against the Forecast backbone's **339 channels on N320** (18 IASI
radiances vs 210 IASI principal components), and with roughly half the finetuning steps
(istep 7 640 vs 16 300). Parity from a much smaller and coarser input.

**Do not say:** "JEPA is better." The effect is ~1% and the two models differ in several ways at
once. "Indistinguishable in practice, from far less input" is the defensible claim.

### `F03_2step_regional`

Same two metrics for global / northern / southern hemisphere. Paired differences at +12 h:

| region | ETS diff (JEPA − Fc) | 95% CI | SEEPS diff | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| global | +0.0047 | [+0.0033, +0.0061] | +0.0050 | [+0.0032, +0.0067] |
| northern | **+0.0071** | [+0.0055, +0.0087] | **+0.0098** | [+0.0076, +0.0120] |
| southern | +0.0023 | [+0.0001, +0.0044] | −0.0004 | [−0.0028, +0.0018] |

**Say:** the +12 h edge is a northern-hemisphere effect. In the south the two are a tie — the SEEPS
difference there is not distinguishable from zero. This is worth saying out loud rather than
letting the global number stand unqualified.

Note: the anemoi IMERG store carries values at all latitudes, but IMERG's native observing range is
60S–60N, so mid- and high-latitude scores lean on filled data.

### `F04_2step_pdf`

Rain-rate PDF (log–log) for both runs against IMERG, at +6 h and +12 h, all N320 points pooled.

**Say:** both models reproduce the observed distribution across three decades of rain rate, and the
two curves lie on top of each other — the same "practically tied" result as F02, in a form that
needs no confidence intervals.

Two systematic errors are visible and worth naming before someone asks:

- **Too much light rain.** IMERG is dry (< 0.05 mm/6 h) at **74%** of points; the models are dry at
  only **52–54%**. That is the FBI > 1 from F02, made visible.
- **Too little extreme rain.** Both fall below IMERG above ~10 mm/6 h. That is what MSE training
  does: the conditional-mean optimum is a smoothed field.

### `F05_2step_case_maps`

Three regions × {IMERG, JEPA, Forecast} at +12 h, one initialisation.

The initialisation is chosen automatically as the one with the most observed rain inside the three
boxes, across the 37 matched initialisations — the choice is made on the **observed** field, so it
cannot favour either model.

**Say:** the two predictions are nearly indistinguishable by eye, and both are smoother than IMERG.

## Part 3 — the 8-step model rolled out to 10 days

`z71y2ik8` was trained at **8 forecast steps = +48 h**. Everything past that in these figures is
extrapolation outside the trained regime. All Part 3 figures use the 10 initialisations
2023-06-01T12 → 2023-06-03T18.

### `F06_rollout_skill` — the hero figure

ETS and SEEPS skill from +6 h to +240 h, with the trained horizon shaded, day 6 marked and the
collapse zone shaded in red.

| lead | +6 h | +48 h (trained) | +96 h | **+144 h (day 6)** | +168 h | +192 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ETS | 0.514 | 0.388 | 0.311 | **0.204** | 0.028 | −0.001 |
| SEEPS skill | 0.657 | 0.540 | 0.413 | **0.269** | 0.082 | 0.058 |

**Say:** trained at +48 h, still scoring ETS 0.20 at +144 h — three times the trained rollout —
with a decay that is close to linear the whole way. Then, between day 6 and day 7, it falls off a
cliff.

### `F07_rollout_fbi_bias`

Frequency bias and mean bias vs lead time.

**Say:** FBI holds a stable 1.15–1.45 band all the way to day 6 — the model keeps producing rain at
a realistic frequency, it does not quietly drizzle everything away. After day 6 FBI drops to ~0.05:
the field stops raining altogether. Mean bias tells the same story from the other side.

### `F08_rollout_activity`

Forecast activity (`froct`, mean |change| between consecutive steps) against observed activity
(`troct`), which is flat at ~0.86 mm/6 h.

**Say:** this is why the skill decay is graceful — the forecast is still *evolving*, at 28% of the
observed step-to-step variability at day 6, rather than having frozen into a smooth climatology.
After day 6 it goes to zero: the field becomes static.

Note on why this metric and not an activity plot: `fact`/`tact`/`acc` all need a climatological
**mean**, and both IMERG climatologies on this system carry only the two SEEPS parameters
(`prob_dry`, `light_heavy_threshold`). `froct`/`troct` need no climatology and answer the same
question.

### `F09_rollout_pdf`

Rain-rate PDF at days 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8 against IMERG. Panels past the collapse are tinted red.

**Say:** the distribution is essentially intact at day 6 and gone by day 7 — at day 8 the model
produces nothing above ~10 mm/6 h at all.

### `F10_rollout_map_strip`

Global, one initialisation: IMERG on top, forecast below, at +24 / +72 / +120 / +144 / +192 h.

**Say:** this is the same claim as F06 with no metric in the way. At day 6 the ITCZ, the monsoon and
the mid-latitude storm tracks are all still in the right places. At day 8 the map is empty.

### `F11_rollout_zoom_*`

The same strip for each region, at +24 / +72 / +120 / +144 h.
`maritime` is the strongest of the three; `monsoon` and `natlantic` are spares.

**Say:** at day 6 the rain bands are smoother than IMERG but structurally correct and correctly
placed. Smoothing is expected — the decoder is trained with MSE.

Per region, if asked:

- **`maritime`** — the best of the three. The ITCZ and the island convection are still where IMERG
  puts them at day 6.
- **`monsoon`** — the day-5 and day-6 panels are visibly *wetter* than IMERG over the Bay of
  Bengal. That is the same FBI ≈ 1.35 from F07, seen locally; do not describe it as the model
  "getting the monsoon right" without noting the wet bias.
- **`natlantic`** — the frontal band is the clearest test of whether structure survives, and it
  does through day 6, displaced rather than dissolved.

### `F12_skill_horizon`

How far the model stays above each ETS level.

| threshold | horizon |
|---|---|
| ETS ≥ 0.40 | +36 h (1.5 days) |
| ETS ≥ 0.30 | +96 h (4.0 days) |
| **ETS ≥ 0.20** | **+144 h (6.0 days)** |
| ETS ≥ 0.10 | +156 h (6.5 days) |

Collapse onset, defined as the first lead where FBI drops below 0.8: **+156 h**.

## Caveats slide

- `z71y2ik8` is scored on **10 initialisations spanning 2.25 days** in June 2023 — enough to
  establish the decay shape, not enough for a seasonal skill claim.
- The 2-step comparison uses 37 matched initialisations. Previously circulated 64-sample numbers
  were **not** sample-matched between the two runs.
- SEEPS here is `1 − SEEPS error` (`calc_seeps` returns `1.0 - seeps_error`, `score.py:1393`), so
  higher is better and the curve should fall with lead time.
- ETS / FBI / SEEPS are **means of per-initialisation scores**, not pooled contingency tables —
  consistent with what `weathergen.evaluate` reports.
- The two backbones differ in architecture, input channels and analysis resolution as well as in
  pretraining objective.
- IMERG's native observing range is 60S–60N; the anemoi store carries values everywhere.

## What is not in here, and could be

- **`yz3h0kyn`** — the Forecast-backbone 8-step run, same 10 initialisations, same 40 leads.
  It is the natural baseline for the day-6 claim and its evaluation JSONs and store are already on
  disk. For reference, its ETS at +144 h is **0.093** against `z71y2ik8`'s 0.204, and it drifts dry
  from +96 h rather than collapsing. Adding it to F06/F07/F08 is a small change to
  `rollout_stats()` and the plotting loops.
- A **persistence or climatology reference line**, which would turn "ETS 0.20 at day 6" from a
  number into a skill statement.
